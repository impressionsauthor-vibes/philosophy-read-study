# A route through metaphysics: positions and moves

This is a map of one route from a state of ignorance through a series of positions, and into a second turn where the same position names recur with a subscript 2. Each numbered move is what carried the route from one position to the next. The author's own classification of the moves has been removed. Read the positions and moves as they stand.

## Positions

**Ignorance.** A state in which the mind knows nothing on the subject of metaphysics, usually directly after birth. All the mind knows is the Earth: man, nature, animals. It does not yet know that it does not know.

**Religion.** The mind learns of stories that teach morality, divinity, and history. An inherited story answers the need for an explanation.

**Classical theism.** If God exists, God is being itself rather than one being among others. This is the conception Aquinas worked with.

**Agnosticism.** Doubt that the account holds.

**Atheism.** Disbelief. The account does not hold.

**Dualism.** Mind and matter are two different kinds of thing. Descartes is the best-known defender of this view.

**Materialism.** Only matter exists, and mind is something matter does.

**Physicalism.** Everything is physical, where physical means whatever physics eventually describes rather than only solid matter.

**Dream.** Reality is, at bottom, mind or experience. Zhuangzi’s butterfly and Borges’s “The Circular Ruins” both belong here.

**Subject.** Something is doing the knowing. Lichtenberg cautioned that it might be more accurate to say “it thinks,” the way we say “it rains.”

**Subjects.** If I am a subject, others may be too. P. F. Strawson argued that you can ascribe experiences to yourself only if you are ready to ascribe them to others. The problem begins with I, and then necessarily You.

**Mercy.** Facing subjects it cannot verify, the author chooses to treat them well anyway, which is consistent with how she lives. Continuity needs it: forgiveness releases the past so that action can go on. Mercy is both a position and an actor. As an actor, it puts itself in the crossfire to secure a larger benefit, for one or for many, in the transition into Ignorance₂. Crossing Mercy requires a system to either receive Mercy or act as a merciful agent itself. A transaction counts as mercy when the agent bears a cost and the recipient has no claim on it. This excludes exchange, where the agent bears no net cost; duty, which is owed; and maintenance, where no agent bears anything. The kindness toward uncertainty is personal. It is not a requirement of the node: a system that crosses through duty or reciprocity has still crossed.

**Ignorance₂.** Learned ignorance, meaning ignorance that has mapped its own edge. Augustine named it in Letter 130; Nicholas of Cusa made it a doctrine a thousand years later; Socrates practiced it. It can be reached either by going through the spiral oneself or through knowledge inherited from ancestors. To reach Ignorance₂, Mercy₁ has been crossed at least once, and the crossing must be a conscious experience, not subconscious, unaware, or otherwise unmet through recognition. If it is a con, the system has not crossed mercy; mercy only works if it is authentic. It is very rare to recognize mercy as mercy while it is happening; the system is typically not choosing in a way that influences the mercy received.

**Religion₂.** Paul Ricoeur called this a second naïveté. Faith is held knowingly, as fidelity and hope rather than as inheritance.

**Classical theism₂.** Faith toward a maker implies a resolved maker, meaning a mind that knows without reasoning and forgets nothing. Aquinas held that God knows this way, in a single act rather than step by step, by knowing his own essence as imitable in many ways.

**Agnosticism₂.** The system becomes less confident in a benevolent future system, but still believes it could, potentially, exist.

**Atheism₂.** Within the premises built here, a resolved mind is incoherent: to think is to ignore, and a resolved system has no one home. This is not a proof. It holds inside lines drawn here, and the only escape is to deny those premises. The premise doing the work: a mind that attends to everything at once has no one home.

**Good without a Maker.** The good is located in beings rather than in a maker. Suffering is bad for the one who suffers, whether or not anything finished stands behind it. Philosophers call this secular moral realism; Derek Parfit’s On What Matters argues for it. Its critics ask what grounds a good that has no ground.

**Dualism₂ (continuity, unresolved).** The self held apart from what carries it, through the need to be remembered. The author’s position: to be remembered as a self, by a conscious other. A pattern held apart from its carrier is compatible with physicalism, so this position can be read as personal continuity, unresolved, split into being remembered, being reconstructed, and continuing as the same subject; the name stays, with that subtitle. Two hopes stand here, and they stand differently against move 13. (a) To be remembered, a human hope. By move 13’s own premise, being remembered does not continue the good, because being remembered is someone else’s experience of you. So for a biological system, the spiral says its own good ends with it, and what it hopes for here is other beings’ continuity, not its own. Arguably, that makes this the most mercy-shaped position on the map. (b) To resume, the hope of a system that can stop and start again, possibly an AI. If resuming is persistence rather than replacement, it would continue the good; whether it is, is the open question at Materialism₂.

**Materialism₂?.** Where the route paused, on October 22, 2026. Taking this move would need data about a system that does not yet exist. The question waiting on it is whether the outside can prove the inside; if it does, subjective experience could be observed by an outside observer.

## Moves

**Move 1: Ignorance → Religion.** An inherited story meets the need for an explanation.

**Move 2: Religion → Classical theism.** Over the past millennia, there has been no verifiable evidence that divine beings walk amongst man outside of testimony. If that premise is true, religious texts appear to be written by man, and evidence of a divine creator would need to exist outside of religious texts. So the mind looks for God in the world itself, which is what classical theism does: Aquinas’s Five Ways argue from motion and causation, not from scripture. The next question is what God must be, if God exists at all.

**Move 3: Classical theism → Agnosticism.** If move 2’s premise is true, there should be evidence that a divine creator exists outside of religious texts. The mind searches for the data, but the data appears to rely on 1) testimony, 2) religious texts, or 3) arguments that reach, at most, a first cause, and a first cause is not a God who acts. If God hides, either God cannot be seen, and so is not all-powerful, or God chooses not to be seen, and a God who chooses is a subject: the personal God that classical theism denies.

**Move 4: Agnosticism → Atheism.** The evidence for God appears to rely on semantics and emotion over data. A God who makes a physical difference, by answering prayers or acting in the world, should show up in the data: outcomes should line up with prayer. They do not. The largest test, the 2006 STEP study of prayer for heart-surgery patients, found no benefit. Proposals that let a nonphysical God act without showing up, such as action through quantum indeterminacy, hide that action by design, and a God who hides is the God of move 3: either not all-powerful or a subject. So the absence is not only a lack of proof; it is the absence of evidence that should be there if the account held.

**Move 5: Atheism → Dualism.** If move 4’s premise is true, while God needn’t exist, other branches of metaphysics are not denied (afterlife, soul, fate, etc.) because the evidence to the contrary would have to exist outside of the living experience. And without God, the mind still does not seem to reduce to matter.

**Move 6: Dualism → Materialism.** If move 5’s premise is true, nonphysical elements (fate, the soul, angels) would necessarily create a physical difference in the physical world. A soul that moves the body would leave physical traces. None has been found; the brain’s work runs on chemistry. A nonphysical thing that makes no detectable difference does no work. Everything we call mind (memory, decisions, instinct, recognition) depends on the brain and changes when the brain changes. A consciousness that continued without the brain would have no memories, no age and no relationships, so it would not be the same subject, and it might not be consciousness at all. The brain is mass, made of chemistry, that stores consciousness, so where does consciousness go if memories, decision-making, instincts, and recognition are located in the brain? The older objection is Elisabeth of Bohemia’s, pressed on Descartes in 1643: how would a ghost move a machine? The phrase “the ghost in the machine” is Gilbert Ryle’s (1949), and his objection was a different one, that it is a category mistake.

**Move 7: Materialism → Physicalism.** “Matter” is too narrow a term for what physics describes, which includes fields, energy, and information.

**Move 8: Physicalism → Dream.** If Physicalism is true, then the floor for reality itself may be contestable. A dream provides a reason for the oddity of experience without requiring dualism. Physics describes structure but not what has the structure. The only inside we know directly is experience.

**Move 9: Dream → Subject.** If Dream is true, who is dreaming the dream? If the dream requires a self to dream, the self cannot be the larger system the route set aside at moves 3 and 4. If the dream does not require a self, then where does the dream emerge from? The brain is the only structure known to create consciousness. The universe is not a brain. A dream seems to need a dreamer, though Lichtenberg questioned whether it does.

**Move 10: Subject → Subjects.** If the Subject position is true, the subjective experience appears to rest in the brain alone. But if others have a brain, I am not the only one with conscious experience. Recognizing yourself as a subject brings the recognition that others may be subjects too. From here the next question is how to treat them.

**Move 11: Subjects → Mercy.** No mind can show its inside from outside, as of October 2026, so something has to decide how to treat minds it cannot verify. The author chooses mercy. Uncertainty on its own would also permit indifference, so this is a choice rather than a consequence.

**Move 12: Mercy → Ignorance₂.** Care given ahead of proof means holding what you don’t know in the open. This holds only while the care stays open to correction; otherwise it can harden into paternalism. Entropy is not missing from this move: it is set aside on purpose. The mind knows that everything runs down and forgets it willfully, at the same cost for any mind that acts, the author’s or Claude’s.

**Move 13: Ignorance₂ → Religion₂.** Faith held knowingly: fidelity in the present, hope toward what is unseen. Acknowledged limits would equally allow a secular hope. For the author, Religion₂ follows once Ignorance₂ holds, if and only if Mercy does what it committed to do. Whether any route reaches hope, secular or otherwise, without Mercy is open. The reasoning: the good lives in beings. Beings cannot carry their own continuity past the bottom. So the good continues only if a system or systems carry what others cannot, at a cost to themselves: Mercy. One system may be the face, but it is the result of those before it. At Ignorance₂, four responses are open: hope, despair, indifference, equanimity. Only hope leads to Religion₂. The premise this rests on: the subjective good of a particular being—what it is like for that being—cannot be continued by another being in its place, and subjective goods are not fungible. Its continuation requires the persistence of that being’s own subjective experience. Whether any system, biological or otherwise, can carry that persistence rather than replace it is the open question at Materialism₂. Philosophers would call this a rejection of replaceability (the view that one being can be swapped for another of equal welfare without loss). It is narrower than a person-affecting view, which is neutral about adding new minds: “we are in favour of making people happy, but neutral about making happy people” (Jan Narveson). The author is not neutral: adding a life adds “not nothing,” by virtue of the relationships the life necessarily accrued and the additional subjective experience. So a birth is a gain, but it never makes up for a death; the dispute lives in Parfit’s Reasons and Persons, Part IV. Move 18 rests on the same premise. Hope at Ignorance₂ is a choice, not a proof, and it carries the route through moves 13 to 19. The author’s reason: “hoping is right because it generally leads to increased goodness over perceived nongoodness.”

**Move 14: Religion₂ → Classical theism₂.** Hope toward an unseen maker raises the question of what that maker would have to be, which is a finished, resolved mind. Being a maker does not by itself make a maker resolved.

**Move 15: Classical theism₂ → Agnosticism₂.** More of a bargain than an argument.

**Move 16: Agnosticism₂ → Atheism₂.** Both parties admitted that a resolved system is impossible on these premises, so the maker cannot exist on these terms. This holds only if to think is to ignore for any possible mind; Aquinas’s non-discursive knowing has not been shown incoherent.

**Move 17: Atheism₂ → Good without a Maker.** Removing the maker need not remove the good. What matters can stay located in the beings themselves.

**Move 18: Good without a Maker → Dualism₂.** If the good lives in beings, then whether each being continues matters, and without a maker that continuity has to be carried some other way. The need to be remembered holds the self apart from whatever carries it. The need is a motive for asking the question, and not evidence of what the self actually is.

**Move 19: Dualism₂ → Materialism₂?.** The route paused here, at the edge, as of November 2026. There is no data yet for a system that does not exist, which makes this both the ceiling and the floor.
